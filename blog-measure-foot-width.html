How do I measure foot width at home?

Use A4 paper, a pen and a ruler to estimate the widest part of each foot. The result adds useful context, but it does not determine shoe fit by itself.

The short answer

Direct answer

Stand on A4 paper, mark the widest points of your forefoot with the pen held vertically, then measure the straight-line distance between those marks in millimetres.

Measure both feet. If they differ, use the larger result as fit context and make sure the smaller foot still feels secure.

What you need

A4 paperOne sheet for each foot is easiest.
Pen or pencilKeep it vertical while marking.
Ruler or tapeUse millimetres for clearer comparison.
Usual socksWear the type used with the shoe.

Five simple steps

1. Prepare a hard, flat surface

Place the paper on a floor that will not compress. Avoid thick carpet.

2. Stand naturally

Place one foot on the paper and distribute your weight normally across both feet. Measuring while seated may give a different result.

3. Mark the widest points

Identify the widest area across the forefoot. Hold the pen upright and mark each side without angling under the foot.

4. Measure in millimetres

Measure the straight-line distance between the two marks. Record the number and the time of day.

5. Repeat for the other foot

Feet are often slightly different. Measure both rather than assuming they match.

How to improve consistency

Repeat the measurement twice and compare the results. Keep the same socks, stance, pen angle and time of day. If the readings differ significantly, repeat rather than averaging uncertain marks.

For shoe selection, combine width with length, toe shape, heel security and how the shoe feels during movement.

What this measurement cannot tell you

Foot width alone cannot predict exact shoe fit. Brands use different lasts, internal volumes and width labels. Materials and construction also change how a shoe feels.

A home measurement is an estimate. BadgerFit can help organise this information, but it does not replace an in-person fitting or professional assessment when one is needed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I measure both feet?

Yes. Measure both and use the larger result as fit context while checking that the smaller foot remains secure.

What time of day should I measure?

Measure at a consistent time that reflects when you usually wear the footwear. Feet may feel different after long periods of standing or activity.

Does width determine my shoe size?

No. Shoe size usually centres on length. Width, shape and internal volume must be considered separately.
