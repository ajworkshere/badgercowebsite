Research & Evidence

What we know.
What we still need to prove.

BadgerFit uses established measurement ideas and clearly labels its own outputs as estimates or beta features. External evidence explains why footwear fit matters; it does not validate BadgerFit itself.

Why Fit Matters

A size number cannot describe the whole foot.

1969

India's existing footwear sizing standard was based on European and French standards.

DPIIT / PIB, 2021
63–72%

Four studies in a review found footwear incorrectly fitted by length, width or both.

Buldt & Menz, 2018
58%

The median share wearing shoes that were too narrow across studies reporting width-specific results.

Peer-reviewed review

The cited studies used different populations and fitting methods. These figures provide context only and should not be treated as BadgerFit accuracy or effectiveness claims.

Current Method

How BadgerFit produces guidance.

Foot scan
A guided, top-view image uses an A4 sheet as a size reference. Classical computer vision estimates foot length and width.
Arch estimate
A footprint-based estimate adds context but does not replace pressure measurement, gait assessment or clinical evaluation.
Fit Check
A deterministic rule system turns answers about one uncomfortable shoe into practical, educational next steps.
Badger Cluster
A beta grouping based on available width and arch information. Its boundaries are still being evaluated.
Research Standard

Evidence before confidence.

Label uncertainty

Measurements remain estimates, and beta outputs remain visibly labelled until validation supports stronger language.

Separate consent

Research participation and diagnostic-data capture should be distinct choices, explained in plain language.

Validate before manufacturing

App patterns should not become footwear standards until sample size, repeatability and real-world outcomes are sufficient.

Current Limits

What BadgerFit does not measure.

The current scan is not a 3D scan, pressure assessment, gait analysis or medical examination. It does not directly measure instep girth, foot volume or heel width, and it does not diagnose an injury or condition. Persistent, severe or unusual discomfort needs assessment from an appropriate healthcare professional.
